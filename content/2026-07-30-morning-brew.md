## 1. This self-hosted app fixed my messy ebook collection overnight — by xda-developers.com

![xda-developers.com](https://static0.xdaimages.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/wm/2025/10/booklore-self-hosted.jpg?w=1600&amp;h=900&amp;fit=crop)

- **Karakeep doc:** `axwzss4zcp51ffqhn1bdqnui`

The author has spent a decade amassing a sprawling ebook-hoard — ePubs, PDFs, Mobi, Humble Bundle leftovers — living in folders and subfolders. Calibre was tried and dismissed as: extremely dated and bloated. Enter **Booklore**, a self-hosted, Docker-based book manager purpose-built to be modern, snappy, and focused rather than an everything-app.

Box: Point it at your library folder and it ingests hundreds/thousands of books automatically, matching files against internal databases for accurate titles, authors, and — the killer feature — high-quality cover art. A wall of covers beats a list of filenames for rediscovering books you forgot you owned. Metadata hits the sweet spot: publication years and synopses without Calibre's genre-dump, and you can dial how much it pulls. Series group properly, and there's a bookshelves concept (genre / read / to-read) that beats flat collections. Multi-user support, reading-progress tracking. Built-in **OPDS server**: punch in the URL and login on your ereader/tablet and scroll the whole library plus your shelves — no cable-and-sync dance.

**Why it's worth your time:** if you hoard ebooks and hate Calibre's UI, this fixes exactly the part Calibre is famous for being bad at — looking good and organizing well — plus OPDS is the genuine "Jellyfin for ebooks" payoff. It's not a Calibre power-user replacement (conversion/ecosystem gap), but for a self-hoster who just wants their library readable again, it's a clean win.
