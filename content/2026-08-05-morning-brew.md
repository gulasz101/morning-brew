## 1. How Cloudflare enforces engineering standards using AI
- **Source:** https://blog.cloudflare.com/engineering-standards-enforcement/
- **Publisher:** The Cloudflare Blog
- **Karakeep doc:** `ok9p86w6olqqjuk6mdwybgkn`

Cloudflare rebuilt its scattered engineering guidance — docs, repo files, chat threads, and the collective head-memory of engineers who keep quitting — into a single governed corpus they call the **Cloudflare Codex**. Their pitch: engineers spent too long hunting for guidance instead of actually working, and couldn't tell if a found standard was current, authoritative, or even applicable. So they centralized it into RFC-style standards (RFC 2119 `MUST`/`SHOULD` keywords, domain-owned, Astro-powered internal site), and now have three AI agents consuming it.

The **AI code reviewer** evaluates merge requests for Codex compliance. Since earlier this year it's flagged ~230,000 violations and withheld approval on ~16,000 of them (the ones tied to enforced MUST statements). Because a full reviewer run takes a couple minutes and engineers bitched about the round trip, they added two escape hatches: **Codex-aligned linters** (millisecond feedback, TypeScript on oxlint first, Rust and Go coming) and a **local CLI** running the same OpenCode-based agents against your diff without the CI leg.

The **spec reviewer** checks technical designs *before* you write the code — runs as a Cloudflare Worker, D1 for state, AI Gateway for models, Cron Trigger to find new specs. Since May 2026 it has reviewed ~600 unique specs across 3,200+ invocations; the breakdown is 65% "major" / 29% "minor" / 6% "critical" findings.

The **incident report reviewer** applies the same trick to postmortems — checks completeness and whether the report explains what happened, names contributing factors, documents resolution, and proposes real follow-ups. 200+ reports assessed since May; 93% were low-impact/internal-only/declared preemptively (of course they were, that's the boring majority). For high-severity incidents the review is now **mandatory** — a report isn't considered complete until all findings are addressed.

The clever bit: rather than shoveling 60+ RFCs wholesale into an LLM and blowing the context window, they have an agent that **extracts and compacts the MUST/SHOULD statements into JSON** with stable slugs and metadata for lazy discovery. So the models only load the RFCs they actually need.

**Verdict:** This is Cloudflare's internal engineering blog flirting with itself, but the *enforced vs. approved* lifecycle distinction is genuinely smart — it gives teams time to absorb new rules before the robot starts blocking their merges. The code-reviewer-as-blocker + linter escape hatch combo is the kind of pragmatic design most "AI enforces our standards" blog posts skip. Skepticism caveat: all numbers are self-reported, and "flagged violations" ≠ "good code" — 230k flags could just mean 230k false positives they've learned to ignore. Still, the structure is worth stealing.
